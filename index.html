A Washing-Machine Month: Calm on the Surface, Turbulent Beneath

July delivered the S&P 500's first down July since 2014 — but the headline barely hints at the turmoil underneath. Semiconductor stocks suffered their worst month since 2002, momentum strategies cratered, and oil surged 22% on renewed Iran tensions. Yet the equal-weight S&P 500 outperformed the Nasdaq-100 by a record 7.6 percentage points, Q2 earnings grew at the fastest pace since 2021, and value continued to outpace growth. The broadening thesis is alive — it just got messier.

Monthly Recap
July 2026 — Major Market Drivers
Chip Stock Crash

Semiconductors Suffer Worst Month Since 2002

The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) plunged -22.1% in July — its worst month since December 2002 — erasing weeks of gains in days. Sandisk fell -46.6%, leading S&P 500 decliners. Yet the ETF remains +67.7% YTD, and the AI infrastructure buildout thesis is intact. The sell-off reflects positioning unwinds, not fundamental deterioration.

Record Rotation

Equal Weight Smokes Nasdaq-100 by 7.6 Points

The Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF gained +1.1% in July while the Nasdaq-100-tracking QQQ dropped -6.6% — a record 7.6 percentage point outperformance dating back to 2003. The S&P 500 Low Volatility Index beat the High Beta Index by 13 points, the 12th-best month in 26 years.

Oil & Geopolitics

Iran Tanker Attacks Send Brent Surging 22%

Brent crude rallied ~22% in July to ~$90/bbl after Iran's IRGC attacked two tankers in the Strait of Hormuz under U.S. military escort. The resumption of hostilities lifted energy to the top-performing S&P 500 sector at +12.6%. Oil's surge pushed the 10-year Treasury yield up 33 bps to 4.74%, its highest level of the year.

Earnings Season

Q2 Blended Growth at 47.4% — Fastest Since 2021

Q2 earnings season delivered the strongest blended year-over-year growth rate since Q2 2021 at 47.4%. About 85% of S&P 500 companies beat estimates by an average of 9%. Microsoft posted its best day since 2008, adding a record $450 billion in market cap. Yet strong results were overshadowed by concerns about AI hyperscaler capex sustainability.

Federal Reserve

Warsh Holds Rates; 30-Year Yield Hits 19-Year High

The FOMC held rates at 3.50%–3.75% at the July 29 meeting. Three regional presidents dissented in favor of a 25 bp hike. The 30-year Treasury yield spiked to 5.24%, its highest in 19 years. Markets now price a 63% probability of a September hike. Chair Warsh committed to fighting inflation but gave no clear forward guidance.

Momentum Unwind

Momentum Factor Posts Worst Month Since 2009

The momentum factor fell roughly 25% in July — its worst performance since 2009 — as the most crowded and leveraged equity positions became the natural source of liquidity. The damage was concentrated in former winners: semiconductors, memory, optical networking, and AI infrastructure names. The Mag-7 actually rose +2.1%, helped by late-month earnings strength.

Sector Performance

Energy & Financials Lead; Tech Lags

Energy led all S&P 500 sectors with +12.6% as geopolitical tensions drove oil higher. Financials gained +6.2%, followed by the defensive trio of Real Estate (+2.5%), Health Care (+2.4%), and Consumer Staples (+2.1%). Technology (-3.4%) underperformed as AI stocks gave back gains. Value vastly outperformed growth across both U.S. and international markets.

Fixed Income

Bonds Sell Off as Yields Surge

The Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate Bond Index returned -1.3% in July as Treasury yields rose across the curve. The 10-year climbed 33 bps to 4.74%. Investment-grade corporates underperformed at -1.5%, while high yield was relatively resilient at -0.3%. The bond market is no longer hedging equities — both duration and discount rates are rising together.

International

Developed Markets Outperform; EM Hit by Tech Selloff

MSCI EAFE gained +1.97% in July, outperforming the S&P 500 for the second straight month. Developed markets benefited from the rotation away from U.S. tech. MSCI Emerging Markets fell -3.03% as the same chip-stock selloff that hit U.S. markets weighed heavily on South Korean and Taiwanese equities, which dominate the EM index.

H2 2026 Outlook
How We're Reading the Second Half of 2026
A Four-Part Framework. We've organized our thinking the way we talk with clients — from what markets did, to what the rules now are, to what to actually do, to what we're watching next. July's data reinforces our strategic tilts: the broadening beyond mega-cap tech is accelerating, value continues to outpace growth, and the geopolitical risk premium is real.
01 · What Happened

The Broadening Accelerated — Even as Tech Cracked

July delivered the S&P 500's first down July since 2014, but the equal-weight index gained +1.1% while the Nasdaq-100 fell -6.6% — a record 7.6-point outperformance. Value beat growth. Energy and Financials led. The rotation away from concentrated mega-cap tech is not just continuing; it's accelerating.

02 · What Changed

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act Reshaped the Tax Landscape

The legislation made permanent estate exemptions, confirmed 2026 tax brackets, and updated contribution limits. The rules for 2026 are now set, creating a planning window for clients in higher wealth tiers. Our advisory conversations are shifting from uncertainty to action.

03 · What to Do Now

Ten Planning Opportunities for a Broadening Market

Higher rates, a more tax-aware environment, and a market rotating toward value and cyclicals create distinct planning opportunities. From Roth conversion strategies to rebalancing out of concentrated mega-cap positions, now is the time to act with the updated legislative backdrop in place.

04 · What We're Watching

Oil, Inflation, the September FOMC & AI Capex ROI

The August 7 jobs report and August 12 CPI are the last major data points before the September 15–16 FOMC meeting, where a 25 bp hike is 63% priced. Oil's direction will determine whether inflation re-accelerates. AI capex sustainability — questioned after the chip crash — will determine whether tech leadership returns or the rotation persists.

Current Asset Class Tilts & Rationale
Our tilts reflect a forward-looking view anchored in the broadening market story, a more hawkish rate environment, and selective international opportunity. July's data — record equal-weight outperformance, the chip crash, and the oil surge — reinforces rather than undermines our positioning.

U.S. Small & Mid Cap Equities
Overweight
Russell 2000 pulled back -3.08% in July but remains +18.11% YTD. The equal-weight S&P 500 outperformed the Nasdaq-100 by a record 7.6 points. Small caps are less exposed to the AI capex unwind and benefit from the broadening rally. Valuations remain attractive relative to large caps.
Emerging Market Equities
Overweight
MSCI EM fell -3.03% in July as the chip-stock selloff hit South Korea and Taiwan hard, but remains +15.9% YTD. The AI semiconductor demand story is intact despite the positioning unwind. Dollar weakness (-1.31% in July) is a tailwind. We maintain the overweight but acknowledge near-term volatility.
U.S. Large Cap Value
Overweight
Value continued to outpace Growth in July. The S&P 500 Low Volatility Index beat the High Beta Index by 13 percentage points — the 12th-best month in 26 years. Financials (+6.2%) and Energy (+12.6%) led. The equal-weight index's record outperformance validates the value tilt.
Developed International (MSCI EAFE)
Neutral
EAFE gained +1.97% in July, outperforming the S&P 500 for the second straight month. YTD return of +9.80% is constructive. Dollar weakness (-1.31% in July) is now a tailwind for USD-denominated returns. European earnings breadth is improving. We are closer to upgrading to overweight.
U.S. Large Cap Growth / Mega-Cap Tech
Underweight
The Nasdaq fell -3.20% in July and the SOXX semiconductor ETF plunged -22.1% — its worst month since 2002. The Mag-7 bounced +2.1% on late-month earnings, but momentum strategies suffered their worst month since 2009. We maintain underweight on mega-cap concentration risk.
Investment Grade Fixed Income
Neutral
The 10-year yield surged 33 bps to 4.74% in July — the highest of the year. The 30-year hit a 19-year high of 5.24%. The Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate returned -1.3%. With three FOMC dissenters favoring a hike, we maintain neutral duration and prioritize credit quality.
High Yield / Credit
Underweight
High yield was relatively resilient at -0.3% in July vs. -1.3% for the Aggregate, but spreads remain tight. With the Fed signaling potential hikes and oil-driven inflation risk, asymmetric downside risk persists. We favor quality over yield.
Gold & Commodities
Overweight
Gold gained +1.93% in July to $4,104.30 as renewed Iran tensions lifted safe-haven demand. YTD return of -5.08% reflects the sharp correction from January's record high, but central bank physical demand provides a durable floor. With geopolitical risk elevated and real yields rising, gold serves as a critical portfolio hedge.
Real Estate (REITs)
Neutral
REITs gained +2.6% in July, benefiting from the rotation into defensive and value-oriented sectors. YTD return of ~14% is strong. The higher-for-longer rate environment (10-year at 4.74%) caps near-term upside, but data center and industrial REITs benefit from AI infrastructure demand. Watch September FOMC for re-rating catalyst.
